THE EXPLAINER Why this happened · What changed · What next
01 · WHY THIS HAPPENED

The Structural Background

Special Intensive Revisions inherit prior rolls, historical litigation, and statutory frameworks. In Bihar, the last intensive door-to-door enumeration took place in 2003. When the 2025 revision began, millions of unverified entries required physical verification under Article 324 and the 1950 Registration of Electors Rules.

02 · WHAT CHANGED

The Operational Development

Following draft roll publications, the Election Commission ordered special enrolment and correction drives across completed-SIR jurisdictions. Elector re-inclusion is driven by Form 6 submissions: in Goa, 88 of 97 omitted voters were promptly restored upon statutory verification.

ATTRIBUTED / PROCEDURAL NOTE
03 · WHAT NEXT

The Unresolved Horizon

An expert committee is reviewing ECINET software role-based access for statutory compliance. The national count of 13 crore draft-roll absences remains an administrative metric that does not establish wrongful exclusion without individual claim audits.

THE DECISION CHAIN

The voter-roll dispute is really a chain of decisions.

A voter list looks like a table of names. Administratively, it is a system—and every stage has a different responsibility.

Article 324 places superintendence, direction and control over electoral-roll preparation with the Election Commission. Statutory rules shape the process; field officers collect information; registration officers handle individual enrolment decisions; courts remain part of the review landscape.

The present argument sits on top of the Bihar SIR ordered in 2025 and considered by the Supreme Court in May 2026. The later national dispute is practical: roll changes, missing names, documentation, software, notices, and remedies available to people left out.

“What happened to each category of name, under which rule, through which officer, and with what remedy?”
